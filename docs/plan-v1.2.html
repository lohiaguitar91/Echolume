v1.2 · Motes that matter
Disha's playtest + the boss-cadence direction · August 16, 2026

0Two inputs, one problem

This doc merges a 10:38 voice memo (Disha, depths 8–18 plus the Abyss, unscripted, ending on "overall looks good") with the direction you and Disha worked out afterwards: bosses and challenges on a fixed cadence, with motes deciding how hard they are.

They turn out to be the same problem from two sides. The memo's complaint list is almost entirely "the game keeps its goals and rewards to itself." The direction is a way to give motes a reason to exist that the player can feel. One is the diagnosis, the other is the cure, and the cure is bigger than the complaint.

The line from the playtest this direction answers: "Your goal is to achieve three stars for each level, but the incentive of doing that isn't really there." Ten minutes of play and the reward structure never landed. Bosses that read your mote count are the first mechanic that makes collecting them matter later, which is the only kind of mattering that motivates.

Nothing about movement, tap response, or audio came up once in ten minutes — the three things that were painful in earlier rounds. Those are settled.

1The new spine — motes are stored light

Today a mote gives a little glow for a moment and a star at the end. Under this direction a mote is something you carry. The fiction is already sitting there waiting: a creature that cannot see, in a game where light is the resource and singing is the cost. So motes become stored light — sight you banked earlier and can spend later without making a sound.

That framing matters because it makes the boss effect obvious instead of arbitrary. You are not spending "points." You are arriving at the most dangerous thing in the game either carrying light or not.

The cadence

Every 7th depth is a gate. Every second gate is a full boss.

Good news on fit: depths 21 and 28 already have leviathan bosses, and depth 14 is already written as chapter 1's finale ("The throat of the deep. Everything it knows, it sends."). The cadence you described is most of the way built. What is genuinely new is depth 7, the mote bank, and the gate screen.

The bank, and the gate

Entering a gate, the game totals the motes you hold from the seven depths behind it and states plainly what that buys. If you arrive light, it says so and offers you the door back:

Arriving heavy. The gate says exactly what you bought.
Arriving light. The warning, stated as a fact rather than a scold.

The rule that keeps this from being a wall: every gate must remain beatable at zero motes by a good player. The bank buys margin, never permission. The moment a boss becomes mathematically impossible without farming, this stops being motivation and becomes a grind gate — the exact thing we ruled out when we dropped gems.

2What the light actually buys

You said the effect should differ per boss. Agreed, and it is what keeps the seventh depth from feeling like the same fight in a new skin. One shared currency, a different translation each time, always built out of mechanics the game already has:

Every one of those is a lever that already exists in the sim. None adds an inventory, a shop, or a screen the player has to learn. That is deliberate: the point is that motes finally do something, not that the game grows an economy around them.

Question 1 — one boss effect, or many?

A
Per-gate effects, as in the table. Each encounter translates light differently, so the cadence stays fresh and each gate teaches something. More design and copy per gate. My pick — it is the version you described, and it is the reason to keep playing.
B
One universal effect everywhere (silent pulses), tuned by amount. Far simpler to build, balance and explain. The 7th depth starts to feel samey by the fourth one.
C
B for the launch cadence (7 and 14), A from 21 onward once the pattern has proven itself. The cautious path if you want this in the next build rather than the one after.

How the bank is counted

This decides whether the idea feels motivating or exhausting, so it is worth being explicit.

Question 2 — what happens to the bank

A
Your best run of each depth counts, permanently. The bank is the sum of your best mote haul on each of the seven depths behind the gate. Replaying a depth you rushed raises it; nothing is ever consumed or lost. Failing the boss costs nothing but the attempt. My pick. Replay becomes a choice rather than a chore, and it can never punish you twice.
B
The bank is spent at the gate and must be re-earned for the next one. More tension and more weight per boss. Also the version where a bad boss run sends you back to re-farm seven levels, which is the classic way this mechanic turns into a grind.
C
Spent on use, but only what you actually spend. Arrive with 96, use 2 of 4 pulses, keep the rest. Middle ground; harder arithmetic for a player to hold in their head.

The colour collision, now that motes are the currency

Confirmed in code

05:16 "I don't really know what these green motes are."

I went looking for a "green mote" expecting heart motes. It isn't those — those are pink. What Disha saw were ordinary amber motes, repainted by their own chain streak. At a chain of 4, drawGame recolours the entire mote field to the chain tier, and that tier is #5effc2 — the exact value of PALETTE.vent, the level exit. The tutorial meanwhile teaches "the green vent takes you deeper."

Depth 13 at chain 4. Both glowing dots are ordinary amber motes.
The ladder's third step lands exactly on the exit's colour.

Recommendation: keep the amber core at every tier and let the chain speak through a ring, a size step and a pulse rate around it; and drop #5effc2 from the ladder so no object ever borrows the exit's colour. The chain stays readable across the field, and a mote always looks like a mote. This was worth fixing before. With motes as the currency it is not optional.

4Stars, now that motes have a job

06:36 "The depth screen just kind of tells you what you achieved. I guess your goal is to achieve three stars for each level, but the incentive of doing that isn't really there."

Today a level grades three things: reach the vent, gather a fraction of the motes, finish under a song budget. Under the new direction the middle one stops being a grade and becomes the bank. That is a real overlap, and leaving both in without deciding gives the player two scoreboards for one action.

Worth knowing: the star incentive does already exist and is simply invisible. Chapter 2 opens at 26 of the shallows' 42 stars. Disha walked through that gate at depth 15 and never knew it was there.

Question 3 — do stars survive?

A
Stars stay, but stop grading motes. Two stars: reached the vent, finished under the song budget. Motes get their own readout — a light bar filling toward the next gate. Each action has exactly one scoreboard, and the mote number finally points somewhere. My pick.
B
Keep all three stars and show the bank alongside. Nothing to un-build, and the existing chapter star gates keep working untouched. Busier results screen, and motes still get graded twice.
C
Drop stars entirely; the light bar and the gates are the whole progression. Cleanest single system, biggest change — it re-cuts the depth map and every chapter gate.

Question 4 — the invisible star goal (independent of question 3)

Disha hit this on three separate levels: "it seems to sort of arbitrarily change the number." It isn't arbitrary — the threshold is ceil(motePct × moteTotal) and both inputs vary per level (motePct runs 0.6 to 0.8 across the 28 depths). But it is only ever shown after the level ends, so it reads as random.

A
State the target up front and in the HUD. The intro card lists the conditions; the counter reads against the threshold rather than the field total. My pick. Do this regardless.
B
A, plus flatten motePct to one value per chapter so the fraction is at least learnable.

5Money — ads on the cadence, no packs

Dropping paid level packs simplifies this a great deal, and I think it is right. Packs meant a store, a content gate, per-pack pricing, restore-purchases on two platforms, and a review-risk conversation about paywalled difficulty. The replacement is one interstitial rhythm and one button.

SurfaceRule
InterstitialAfter clearing a gate depth (every 7th), on the win screen only.
NeverAfter a death. After a failed boss. During the gate warning. On first launch.
RewardedOptional revive at a boss, offered once per attempt, never auto-played.
IAPOne non-consumable, remove_ads. Removes interstitials permanently; the rewarded revive stays available as a choice.
ContentEvery depth free, forever. No pack gate at 50.

The cadence lines up neatly: the gate is already a natural pause, the player has just won something, and the ad lands on a high rather than a failure. That placement rule survives this change intact.

What this costs, stated plainly

Packs were the high-spender path; ads plus a single unlock is a lower ceiling per player. In exchange: no content gating, a simpler store presence, one purchase to support instead of many, and no review thread about paying past a difficulty spike. For a first title with no audience I think the trade is clearly right — reach and rating are worth more right now than revenue per player.

Question 5 — ad timing and the revive

A
Interstitial after every gate win, plus a rewarded revive at bosses. Roughly one ad per seven levels, always on a victory. My pick.
B
Interstitial only, no rewarded revive. Cleanest and most respectful; leaves real money on the table, since rewarded video usually out-earns interstitials in this genre.
C
Ads deferred entirely to the release after launch, so the boss cadence ships clean and earns a rating first. This was the original plan's sequencing and it is still defensible.

Question 6 — does the launch build include all of this?

A
Ship the boss cadence and ads together as v1.2, holding the store submission until it is all done.
B
Ship the playtest fixes now as 1.1.1 — copy, colour and two icons, days not weeks — then the boss cadence as 1.2, then ads as 1.3. Three small releases, each verifiable, and the first gets you a live listing and a rating while the rest is built. My pick, and the only question here I feel strongly about.
C
Hold everything and submit once, fully featured. Fewest review cycles, longest wait for any real-world signal.

6The rest of the playtest

None of these interact with the boss work. All are copy, colour, or a few SVG paths, and all could ship this week.

Hints read as flavour, not instruction

05:07 "Is it supposed to be an actual help, or is it just special notes? It's not super clear."

Every hint encrypts its tactical content inside a literary line. "The river runs upstream here" means a current pushes back, so budget your songs — but that translation exists nowhere in the game. By depth 16 Disha had stopped reading them. Proposal: keep the poetic line as the headline, add one dim plain line beneath it. Copy only, and the writing survives.

Two tutorial icons don't read

09:20 "The one next to 'songs are loud' — I don't understand what that icon is. Same with 'gather the amber motes' — what's the green circle in that icon supposed to be?"

The copy worked; the drawings failed. The hunter glyph is a bare 12px line with no arrowhead, so it reads as a connector rather than a chase. The vent glyph is a plain green dot slightly larger than the mote dots beside it — the same collision as above, in the one screen meant to prevent it. Proposal: arrowhead and motion arc on the first; draw the vent as the ringed portal it actually is on the second.

Settings don't explain themselves

10:18 "It just says haptics, or high-contrast palette, or show danger on screen, but it's not clear what that means."

"Show danger on screen" is the accessibility path for deaf and hard-of-hearing players and its name says nothing about that. Proposal: one dim sub-line under each toggle.

Two fades — one is the design, one might be a bug

Reported forty seconds apart, and not the same thing. The idle fade in the Abyss is reveal energy decaying, which is the core fiction and is correct — though a player who suspects the central mechanic is a rendering bug is still a signal, and a one-time nudge would convert the doubt into the lesson. The in-play flicker ("the boundaries of the path aren't there, like for a split second") is the only thing in the memo that sounds like a real defect. Disha hedged it heavily. I would not guess at a fix; it wants a repro first.

Lives — leave alone

You have wondered whether lives mean anything given how rarely you die. Disha lost several, went looking for the rules, and approved of both: "lives do restore after you beat a level, which is good" and "when I die it just continues the same level, so that's good." One data point against tightening them.

7Risks worth naming

  • Grind. The biggest failure mode. Mitigated by question 2A (best-run banking, never consumed) and the zero-mote beatability rule. Drop either and this becomes a farming game.
  • The warning reading as a paywall. "Are you sure?" must state a fact and offer a door, never imply a purchase. There is no way to buy light — the copy must never suggest otherwise.
  • Sawtooth. Gates are peaks by definition, so the curve now has a designed spike every 7. The chapter-opening rule (~60% of the previous peak) has to be measured from the non-gate levels, or depth 29 opens at boss difficulty.
  • Re-verification. Any change to levels.js or draw.js means re-running __echo.verifyAll() across all 28 depths plus npx cap sync. That is the real schedule cost, not the code.
  • This is one playtester. Disha is one person, ten minutes, first time seeing the game. Strong signal on comprehension, weak signal on difficulty and retention.
