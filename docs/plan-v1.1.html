0The first minute — cold open

Our own guardrail says nothing between the player and the water — and then the first launch stacks three screens (About → title → How-to) before Depth 1. The plan's one self-contradiction, found on re-read.

Effort SShip in v1.1 Cold open: a brand-new install fades straight into Depth 1's water, dark and silent, with the story line as an in-world caption: "A small blind creature of the deep, who moves the only way it knows…" — then the first hint: "Tap the water." The first tap is the audio unlock, the first song, and the tutorial's first beat all at once. The in-level hints already teach everything else; the How-to page and About stay in the menu for anyone who wants them, and returning launches keep today's title screen.

Every pre-play screen costs a measurable slice of first-session completion; an atmosphere game's strongest first impression is the atmosphere itself, not text about it.

1Abyss death recap — make the run mean something

Today: dying in the Abyss shows the generic game-over panel: one line of depth, two buttons. The run's story — was it a record? how close was it? what did you gather? — is thrown away, and the strongest retention moment an endless mode has (the "one more dive" screen) is wasted.

Current: a run at full tension… that ends in a two-line menu.
Proposed: full recap — record state, comparison bar, run stats, progress to the next milestone.

Effort SShip in v1.1 Every number shown is already tracked (stats.depth, stats.pings, stats.motes, abyssBestDepth, milestonesFired). This is one new panel and a diff against the saved best, reusing the results-panel styling. "NEW RECORD" gets the mint treatment, the win jingle, and heavy haptics; a near miss ("41 m short") gets its own line — near-misses are the strongest "again" trigger there is. When the Game Center bridge lands, the leaderboard button slots into the third row.

2Mote chains you can see — the color sequence

Today: chaining motes builds a rising melody and (since the last update) widens your glow — but both are easy to miss. The chain has no face. A color sequence gives collection a visible identity: each consecutive mote inside the combo window shifts the chain's color one step, your tail wears the current color, and the melody, the tint, and the counter all say the same thing: keep the chain alive.

Current: all motes are amber; a 7-chain looks identical to seven strays.
Proposed: the ladder is visible — combo count, hue, melody, and aura tell one story.

Effort SShip in v1.1 Implementation is a hue ramp on the existing combo counter (moteCombo already exists, window 2.4s): motes near the player inherit the chain color, the tail particle tint follows, and an ×8 chain fires a silent "bloom" reveal pulse — a real (small) mechanical prize that stays on-theme: light, not noise. The color ladder ends at violet deliberately — it's the hunters' color, "you're glowing bright enough to be interesting." Also raise the per-mote glow bonus from 3% to 4% (same 45% cap) so the existing aura reward is felt sooner.

3Scaling difficulty — new elements, set-piece bosses, and hearts

Depths 1–14 teach one instrument at a time. A second chapter (The Trench, depths 15–28) shouldn't just add more urchins — it should add new listeners. Everything below composes with the one verb; none of it adds a second input.

Current hazard set: urchins, hunters, currents — fully composed by depth 13.
Proposed: five listeners, two leviathans — difficulty from composition, not stat inflation.

Effort M–Lv1.2 content pack Hearts stay at three. Raising punishment is the lazy axis; the fun axis is asking more of the same three hearts. Deeper levels instead hide heart motes (one per level, off the safe path, guarded) — recovery exists but must be earned through the game's own verbs. Boss levels checkpoint at the lair mouth, so their difficulty is honest: the challenge is the room, not the walk back to it. Sound identity scales with the chapter: the Trench sings the same pentatonic system in a darker mode, and each leviathan lair has a signature drone — players should hear how deep they are before they read it.

4The gem question — economy options, honestly compared

Your sketch: motes bank into gems (~100:1), ~10 gems buys a mid-level revive that resumes the run, gems eventually buyable. That's a coherent loop — it's the Heroes-of-History arena pattern. Whether Echolume should run it depends entirely on which product it wants to be:

ModelSession feelRevenueFit for Echolume
Premium, no meta-economy
$2.99, free retries (today)		 Pure; deaths cost only pride One-time, small but honest Perfect fit for an atmosphere game; strongest reviews
Gem revive

Energy / lives gate
5 dives, refill on a timer or gems		 Punishes your most engaged players; kills "one more dive" Monetizes impatience Wrong genre. The #1 review complaint in every game that ships it. Do not build.
Rewarded ads
bubble-popper style: ad = revive / gems		 A 30s ad after a silent cave death is tonal whiplash Best reach if free Only ever opt-in, capped, and never interstitial — and even then it taxes the game's one unfair advantage: its atmosphere

Recommendation — two SKUs, one economy, cosmetics carry the money

  • Ship v1.0/v1.1 exactly as it is: premium, zero economy. That identity ("no ads, nothing tracked by us") is the store differentiator and earns the review score that everything else compounds on.
  • If/when a free SKU ships (v1.2+): run your gem loop with the earn side exactly as sketched (100 banked motes → 1 gem, +1 gem for a flawless-collection level, +1 for daily first clear) — but point the spend side mostly at cosmetics: lume colors, tail styles, song timbres (the synth already supports it). Cosmetics outsell revives in every comparable title, and they can't corrupt difficulty.
  • Revive stays, priced honestly: 10 gems resumes at the death spot with one heart, once per level. Revived runs can't earn stars above what they had — records stay clean, whales stay happy, purists lose nothing.
  • Never: energy gates, interstitial ads, or gems required for base progression. If ads exist at all, one opt-in rewarded slot ("watch to bank +2 gems"), capped at 3/day, never at a death screen.
  • "Consecutive motes across levels" becomes the streak bonus: a flawless-collection level pays a bonus gem and keeps a streak counter running; a missed mote just ends the streak, never subtracts.
The loop, if (and only if) a free SKU ships. Premium stays clean.

5Timed play — fold it into a quest board, add trench medals

A separate timed mode would fragment 14 levels across two lists. Two lighter structures do the same job better:

"Currents": three daily asks across modes — the timed challenge is a quest type.
Trench medal: a per-level par-time chip on results — a fourth chase that never gates progress.

Effort S–MMedals v1.1Currents v1.2 Medals are pure read-side work: every level already records bestTime; add hand-tuned par times to the level defs and a chip on results + level cards. The Currents board needs a screen, a daily seed, and quest templates that read existing run stats — no new gameplay systems.

Reward consistency fix: the mock shows gem rewards, but gems only exist if the free SKU ships. In the premium SKU, Currents pays tide-marks — a cosmetic-only tally that unlocks lume tints and song timbres at fixed counts (same cosmetics catalog the gem economy would sell). If the F2P fork ever ships, tide-marks convert 1:1 into that catalog and quests start paying gems. One reward pipe, two currencies, zero contradiction.

6Flow — the no-disruption audit and the loop that keeps players in

A walkthrough of every transition in the current build, counted in taps, plus the four smallest changes that close the gaps. The bar: nothing between the player and the water, and always one visible reason to go back in.

Flow guardrails (adopt as written rules for every future feature)

  • Nothing modal ever appears during play; all teaching stays in-world (hints, sound, light).
  • Every screen is at most one tap from being back in the water.
  • Audio ducks, never hard-cuts; transitions fade the world, never blank it.
  • Death to retry stays under 1.5 seconds and never shows an upsell in the premium SKU.
  • No timers, energy, or appointment mechanics — the only reason to return is wanting to.
  • Every run ends by pointing at the next one: record state, near-miss distance, tease, or quest progress.

The loop, named

Play → feel (chain colors, glow, melody) → recap that points forward (record / near-miss / medal / tease) → one-tap re-entry (Continue chip). Sections 1, 2, and 5 build the middle of this loop; the four changes above close its ends. That end-to-end shape — not any single feature — is what "can't put it down" actually is.

7Reach — who else gets to stay

Accessibility that fits the fiction

  • Visual threat option: the game speaks danger through audio (heartbeat proximity). A settings toggle adds a soft screen-edge pulse that breathes with the same signal — deaf and hard-of-hearing players get the same dread, drawn in light. Effort Sv1.1
  • Chain colors carry a second channel: the §2 hue ladder always pairs hue with size and pulse-rate steps, so the chain reads without color vision. Adopted as a §2 requirement, not an option.
  • Already true, worth stating on the store page: one-thumb, either hand, no timed inputs outside optional trials, reduced-motion and high-contrast modes.

Friends make depth mean something

When the Game Center / Play Games bridge lands (v1.2), the Abyss recap's comparison line prefers a friend over the global board: "34 m past Disha's best." Beating a stranger is a number; beating a friend is a message you didn't have to send. Platform-provided, zero accounts of our own, consistent with the privacy stance.

Why this is the full set

Three iterations covered the whole player journey with nothing doubled and nothing contradicting: first minute (§0), moment-to-moment feel (§2), the run's ending (§1), the reasons to return (§5, §6), the content that keeps mastery growing (§3), the money question answered without touching any of it (§4), and who else gets to stay (§7) — all under one set of flow guardrails. Anything added past this point is new scope wearing polish's clothes; the next correct step is building v1.1, then letting real players amend the plan.
